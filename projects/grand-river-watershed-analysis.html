Grand River Watershed Analysis

An ArcGIS Pro workflow that builds a watershed view from a 30 m elevation model, then connects terrain-derived drainage with land use and hydrologic soil context.

  • ArcGIS Pro
  • D8 hydrology
  • Terrain analysis
  • Land use
  • 30 mElevation-model cells
  • D8Flow-direction method
  • 17NNAD83 UTM zone

01 / Objective

Build a traceable spatial model of how water moves through the basin.

The assignment combined raster hydrology and regional context. The central task was to turn elevation data into connected flow paths and basin boundaries, then interpret those results alongside land-cover and soil information.

I organized the work as a sequence of derived datasets so that each map could be traced back to the input elevation model and processing choice.

02 / My work

I completed the GIS workflow and assembled the analytical maps.

  • Prepared a 30 m digital elevation model in NAD83 UTM Zone 17N and generated supporting terrain views.
  • Calculated D8 flow direction and flow accumulation.
  • Applied a stream threshold, linked the extracted network, and delineated watershed and subwatershed areas.
  • Compared land-use information and hydrologic soil groups and evaluated drainage density as a basin descriptor.

03 / Workflow

Terrain first, interpretation second.

  1. Prepare elevation. Project, clip, inspect, and condition the DEM for hydrologic processing.
  2. Route flow. Derive D8 direction and accumulated upstream area for every raster cell.
  3. Extract drainage. Convert high-accumulation cells into a connected stream network.
  4. Delineate basins. Use the drainage structure to define watershed and subwatershed boundaries.
  5. Add context. Compare the terrain-derived result with land use, soils, hillshade, and drainage-density mapping.

04 / Output

A coherent set of maps, with draft conclusions kept separate from finished evidence.

The analysis produced terrain, stream, watershed, subwatershed, land-use, and soil-context outputs. The subwatershed map demonstrates the regional structure particularly well: upstream branches combine through the central basin before the Grand River reaches Lake Erie.

The supporting report still contains unfinished numerical fields. This page therefore presents the completed GIS workflow and map evidence without turning draft calculations into final findings.
