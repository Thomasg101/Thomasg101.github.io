Envelope Thermal-Bridge Studies

Two-dimensional models used to compare insulation continuity at difficult roof-to-wall and balcony-slab junctions without losing sight of the existing construction.

  • Flixo
  • Ubakus

  • Isotherms

01 / Context

Continuity becomes hardest where geometry changes.

Roof edges, structural penetrations, and exposed balcony slabs can bypass otherwise effective insulation. Simple centre-of-wall R-values cannot show how those junctions redistribute heat or affect local interior-surface conditions.

I studied two detail families: an existing steel roof/truss connection at a masonry wall and a balcony-slab connection with alternative interior and exterior insulation strategies.

02 / My work

I translated construction details into comparable thermal models.

  • Reconstructed the principal materials, structural members, and insulation layers in Flixo.
  • Created consistent existing, partial-insulation, full-insulation, interior-insulation, and exterior-insulation scenarios where applicable.
  • Reviewed heat-flow paths and temperature contours at the critical transitions.
  • Used a separate Ubakus wall-assembly check to support the broader thermal and moisture review.

03 / Comparison

Change one envelope decision at a time.

  1. Establish the existing geometry. Preserve the structural path and known assembly layers.
  2. Define scenarios. Compare worst-case, partial, and continuous roof/wall insulation paths.
  3. Test balcony options. Contrast two-inch interior and four-inch exterior insulation arrangements and a drop-ceiling assumption.
  4. Read the fields. Review how isotherms and heat flow concentrate around steel, slab edges, and interrupted insulation.

04 / Value

The models turn a detail debate into a visual comparison.

Rather than treating each proposed layer in isolation, the scenario set shows whether the insulation path actually remains continuous around the junction. That makes thermal bypasses and local surface-temperature concerns easier to communicate to a project team.

No final project recommendation or approved numerical transmittance result was present in the public-use archive, so this case study documents the comparative method rather than claiming a selected design.
